About me

My work sits where imaging, tracking and measurement meet. Day to day, that means computing rigid-body transforms between camera, patient and instrument space, and proving with measured data that the result meets its acceptance criteria.

Read more

I joined the Healthcare Technology Innovation Centre, IIT Madras as a research intern, working on optical tracking for image-guided systems and building methods to verify tracking accuracy. As a project associate I ran QC and ASTM-based validation of navigated spine instruments. Now, as a project engineer, I develop automatic intra-operative registration for spinal surgery using O-arm and 3D C-arm imaging, with laser tracker and CMM measurements as ground truth.

Alongside the engineering, I author the design-control documentation and run the verification protocols that support release of navigated spine and TKA products.

My research roots are in biomechanics. My bachelor's paper used OptiTrack motion capture and force platforms to compare badminton strokes and lunges between trained and untrained players. I play competitive badminton myself and won gold in the men's event at the Open Nationals championship.

3D intra-operative automatic registration for spinal surgery

Project Engineer, IIT Madras. Funded by the Department of Biotechnology

Navigated pedicle screw placement depends on knowing exactly where the patient's vertebrae are relative to the tracking camera. This project automates that registration from intra-operative 3D imaging.

What I do

  • Develop registration pipelines for pedicle screw placement using O-arm and 3D C-arm.
  • Operate the laser tracker and CMM for point capture.
  • Compute rigid-body transforms with Kabsch and ICP to measure spatial accuracy, achieving 0.7 mm target registration error.

Accuracy verification of stereo camera systems

Research Intern and B.Tech final year project, HTIC, IIT Madras. Grade A

Optical navigation is only as good as its camera, and accuracy is not uniform across the working volume. This project set up a repeatable way to measure it.

  • Established measurement and verification methods for optical tracking accuracy in image-guided systems.
  • Built an Accuracy Verification Tool (AVT) using Hough-transform blob detection to compute marker pose across the full working volume.
  • Quantified system accuracy through RMSE pose deviation and wrote a complete Python validation script for working-volume analysis.

Research

I am interested in measuring the human body precisely: how it moves, and where it sits in space. My bachelor's research used optical motion capture to study movement in sport. At IIT Madras, the same ideas of optical tracking, rigid-body transforms and careful accuracy verification keep a surgical instrument aligned with the patient's anatomy. The question I want to keep working on is how to make these measurements accurate enough to act on.

Biomechanical Analysis of Badminton Stroke and Lunge in Trained and Untrained Players

Haris, S., & Celestin Jerald, A. (2026). Proceedings of HWWE 2024, Vol. 4, Lecture Notes in Mechanical Engineering, Springer, Singapore, pp. 61–74. Presented at the HWWE 2024 International Ergonomics Conference.

The study compares how trained and untrained players perform the badminton stroke and lunge, to see how training shows up in both movement and loading.

  • Stroke patterns captured with an OptiTrack 3D motion capture system.
  • Ground reaction forces measured with force platforms during the lunge.
  • Comparative analysis between trained and untrained groups.
